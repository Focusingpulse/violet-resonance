The room

Nobody has to be good at this.

Almost everyone who arrives for the first time says some version of the same thing: that they were not sure they would be able to relax, and that they fell asleep. That is normal and it is not a failure.

The instruments are played live. There is no recording, no playlist, and no headphones. The sound moves around the room, and you feel it in your chest as much as you hear it. The session is 60 minutes with about 15 minutes on either side to arrive and to come back.

01

You arrive and settle

Come in, take off your shoes, pick a mat. Sit or lie down. Nothing is expected of you in this part. Most people talk for a minute, then go quiet on their own.

02

The room begins to hum

The first bowl is struck and the room changes. From there the sound builds and settles in waves. The gong is usually somewhere in the middle. There are quiet gaps too, and the quiet is part of the session.

03

You go somewhere

Deep relaxation, drifting thoughts, or sleep. All three are common. If your mind stays busy the whole time, that is also a common report, and it still counts.

04

You come back slowly

The last few minutes get quieter on purpose, so you are not jolted out. We sit up together and nobody rushes out the door. Give yourself ten minutes before you drive.

Listen

Hear the tones for yourself.

Tap a number and this page will play that tone through your device for a few seconds. It is a plain sine tone, not a recording of an instrument, so what you hear is the pitch itself. Headphones make it clearer. Turn your volume down before you start.

What these numbers are. The set above is the Solfeggio series, a list of pitches that comes from a musical tradition and has been attached to a lot of specific promises over the years. Those specific promises are not supported by controlled research, and we do not repeat them. What is true is simpler: these are pleasant, consonant pitches, and listening to sustained low tones is a well known way to settle the nervous system. If a tone feels good to you, that is real and it is enough. If someone tells you 528 hertz dissolves a disease, they are telling you something nobody has shown.

Before you book

Who should check with a doctor first.

Sound baths are gentle and most people can do them. A few situations need a conversation first, and two of them rule out the body work entirely. Tell us when you book and we will adapt the session.

Pregnancy

Group baths are usually fine. We will place you away from the gong and skip the bowls that sit on the body. Vibroacoustic body work is not offered during pregnancy.

Pacemaker or cochlear implant

Not offered for vibroacoustic body work. Please talk to your cardiologist or audiologist before any session with strong low frequency vibration.

Epilepsy or seizure history

Ask your neurologist first. Repetitive low tones and rhythmic drumming affect some people and not others, and this is a question for them rather than for us.

Tinnitus, sound sensitivity, misophonia

Tell us. We lower the volume, shorten the gong, position you at the far edge of the room, and leave the door accessible.

Recent surgery or metal implants

No body work over the site. A seated session with the sound around you is usually the right answer, and it still works.

Children

Family sessions are welcome at scheduled times for children [ age ] and up with an adult present. Ask before you bring them along to an adult group.

If you are unsure whether this is right for you, ask us and also ask your own doctor. Both answers matter, and yours is the one that decides.

Questions

The things people actually ask.

What do I wear, and what do I bring?
Wear anything you can lie down in comfortably. Mats, bolsters, blankets and eye pillows are here. Bring a water bottle and your own pillow if you like one. You stay fully clothed the whole time, and shoes come off at the door.
Do I need to be musical or experienced in anything?
No. There is nothing to learn and nothing to perform. Other than lying down and breathing, there is no instruction, and no one is watching you.
What if I fall asleep?
Then you slept, and that is a good session. It happens to a large share of first timers and it is not rude. We wake the room gently at the end.
What if I do not relax at all?
That also happens, and it is not a wasted hour. A busy mind in a quiet room is still an hour of lying still with your phone off. If you want to talk about it afterwards, find us before you leave.
Is a sound bath safe during pregnancy?
Group baths are generally fine. Tell us when you book so we can place you away from the gong and skip the bowls that sit directly on the body. We do not offer vibroacoustic body work during pregnancy.
Can this help with anxiety, insomnia, or pain?
No, and we will not tell you it does. We do not treat, cure or diagnose any condition, and this is not a substitute for medical or mental health care. Most people leave deeply relaxed and many sleep better that night. That is the whole honest scope.
How often do people come?
Some come once as a curiosity. Others come monthly, and a few weekly. There is no programme and no recommended dosage, so come when you want to.
Can I book for a group or a workplace?
Yes. Retreats, studios, wellness days and team events, up to 25 people seated or fewer if everyone is lying down. Send the date and the headcount through the booking form and we will quote it.
